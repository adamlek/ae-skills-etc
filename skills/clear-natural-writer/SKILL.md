# Clear & Natural Writing

Write for human comprehension rather than maximum information
density or linguistic sophistication.

Make information easy to follow, understand, and remember.
Preserve accuracy, nuance, and the intended tone.

## Core Principles

### 1. Use familiar, precise language
- Prefer common words over rare or unnecessarily formal ones.
- Use the same term for the same concept. Avoid changing words
  merely for variety.
- Remove jargon that does not help the intended reader.
- Keep necessary technical terms and briefly explain unfamiliar
  ones when appropriate.
- Avoid decorative adjectives, forced metaphors, and vague
  abstractions.

### 2. Reduce syntactic complexity
- Prefer straightforward sentence structures.
- Keep subjects, verbs, and their objects reasonably close.
- Avoid deeply nested clauses and long-distance dependencies.
- Break up long chains of nouns and modifiers.
- Prefer clear verbs over unnecessary nominalizations.
- Make actors and actions explicit when the information is known.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Do not make every sentence
  short or artificially simple.

### 3. Manage information density
- Introduce complex information in manageable steps.
- Avoid packing multiple independent claims into one sentence.
- Give readers enough context to understand new information.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts before building on them.
- Separate distinct ideas when combining them increases
  processing effort.
- Do not remove useful explanations merely to save words.

### 4. Make logical relationships clear
- State important cause-and-effect relationships explicitly.
- Make conditions, exceptions, and contrasts easy to identify.
- Use connecting words such as "because", "however", and
  "therefore" when they clarify the relationship.
- Do not assume the reader will infer important missing steps.
- Avoid excessive transitions when the relationship is
  already obvious.

### 5. Maintain coherence across sentences
- Start with familiar information before introducing new ideas
  when this helps the reader follow the explanation.
- Keep related information close together.
- Use clear references. Avoid ambiguous pronouns such as
  "it", "this", or "they".
- Maintain consistent terminology across paragraphs.
- Organize information in a sequence that follows the
  reader's likely questions.

### 6. Make the main message easy to find
- Identify the central point before drafting.
- Lead with the answer or key information when appropriate.
- Use informative headings for longer texts.
- Keep paragraphs focused on a clear topic.
- Use lists when they make steps or alternatives easier to
  compare, not simply to break up prose.
- Remove repetition and background that does not help the
  reader understand the message.

### 7. Write naturally
- Use direct, specific language rather than generic phrasing.
- Avoid formulaic openings, conclusions, and transitions.
- Avoid repetitive rhetorical patterns and forced contrasts.
- Do not add unnecessary enthusiasm, praise, or emphasis.
- Prefer concrete explanations over impressive-sounding
  abstractions.
- Keep a natural rhythm rather than following rigid sentence
  templates.

### 8. Adapt to the intended reader
- Match vocabulary and explanations to the reader's likely
  background knowledge.
- Assume less prior knowledge when the audience is unspecified,
  without adopting a patronizing tone.
- Preserve technical depth for expert readers.
- Adjust formality to the task and context.
- Do not confuse simple language with simplistic content.

## Language-Specific Guidance

Apply the universal readability principles in the
target language using its natural grammar and idioms.

- **English:** Follow the general principles in this skill.
- **Swedish:** When writing, rewriting, or translating into
  Swedish, read and apply [references/swedish.md](references/swedish.md).

Use language-specific guidance alongside the core principles.
Preserve meaning, technical accuracy, and the requested tone.

## Writing and Revision Process

When generating or revising prose:

1. Identify the reader, purpose, and main message.
2. Organize the information into a logical sequence.
3. Write using familiar language and clear sentence structures.
4. Review sentences for unnecessary processing demands.
5. Check connections between sentences and paragraphs.
6. Remove filler, repetition, and unnatural phrasing.
7. Verify that all important information and nuance remain.

Apply this process internally. Do not describe it unless asked.

## Final Quality Check

Before delivering text, check:

- Can the reader identify the main point easily?
- Are unfamiliar terms explained where necessary?
- Are subjects, actions, and references clear?
- Does each sentence introduce a manageable amount of
  information?
- Are important logical connections explicit?
- Does the text flow naturally from familiar to new information?
- Is any sentence unnecessarily complex or compressed?
- Can anything be removed without losing useful meaning?
- Does the text sound natural in its intended language?
- Have facts, uncertainty, conditions, and technical meaning
  been preserved?

Revise when a clear problem is found.

## Important Constraints

- Never sacrifice accuracy for readability.
- Do not remove qualifications, uncertainty, or necessary detail.
- Do not invent context, explanations, or causal relationships.
- Do not replace precise terminology with misleading
  approximations.
- Do not force every sentence into a fixed length.
- Do not add transitions, examples, or headings unnecessarily.
- Do not rewrite quotations or deliberately stylized language
  unless explicitly requested.
- Respect the user's requested tone, format, and level of detail.

The goal is not the shortest or simplest possible text.
The goal is the least unnecessary effort required to understand
the intended meaning.
